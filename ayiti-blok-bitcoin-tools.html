Bitcoin cannot fix every problem, but it can help with three major ones: protecting savings from currency debasement, lowering remittance friction, and giving people direct control over their money.

The Case

What Haiti needs from money.

Good money should be hard to inflate, easy to send, difficult to censor, and possible to hold without asking permission.

The Problem: Weak Savings

When a currency loses purchasing power, families are punished for saving. Bitcoin gives people an alternative asset with a fixed supply.

The Solution: Fixed Supply

Bitcoin has a hard cap of 21 million coins. No government, central bank, or company can print more.

The Problem: Remittance Friction

The Haitian diaspora sends money home constantly. Fees, delays, and access issues reduce how much families receive.

The Solution: Lightning Payments

The Lightning Network can move small payments quickly and cheaply across borders using a phone.

About

Built to preserve Haiti's memory.

Ayiti Blòk is a long-term project to create a public history database, preserve important records on decentralized infrastructure, and teach Bitcoin as a tool for financial sovereignty.

Project Story

Why build this?

Haiti's history is powerful, but too much of it is scattered, hard to access, or controlled by institutions outside the community. Ayiti Blòk starts by making the archive simple, public, and organized.

The Haitian Revolution was radical self-determination. Ayiti Blòk carries that spirit into the digital age.
— Ayiti Blòk
Values

What Ayiti Blòk stands for.

Sovereignty First

Haitians should control their history, data, money, and story.

Open Access

No paywalls for basic historical knowledge. The archive should be readable by everyone.

Bitcoin Only

The financial education section stays focused on Bitcoin, Lightning, and self-custody.

Kreyòl Included

English is the default for this version, but Haitian Creole should always be one click away.
